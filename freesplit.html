Designing FreeSplit around clarity, fairness and less calculation.

01 / Problem framing

Splitting money sounds simple until the rules arrive.

The product challenge is not just arithmetic. Users need to know who paid, who participated, how the amount should be divided and what needs to happen next.

Where friction appears

Shared meals, trips, subscriptions and group purchases can involve different participants and unequal contributions. A useful product needs to make those rules understandable before presenting a final balance.

That leads to a product principle: reduce the number of decisions a user has to hold in their head at once.

02 / Product logic

Rules should be visible, not hidden.

A strong expense-splitting flow makes the underlying calculation understandable enough that users can trust the result.

01

Capture the group

Define participants before creating a transaction so every amount has a clear set of people attached to it.

02

Capture the payment

Record who paid and the total amount without forcing the user to calculate balances manually.

03

Apply the split rule

Support the core product question: is the amount shared equally, or does the product need to represent a different allocation rule?

04

Explain the result

Translate raw arithmetic into a clear balance so a user can understand what they owe or should receive.

05

Design for edge cases

Think through zero-value entries, missing participants, duplicate inputs and repeated transactions before they become support problems.
